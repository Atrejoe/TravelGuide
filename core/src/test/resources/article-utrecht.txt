Utrecht ( UK: , US: ; Dutch: [ˈytrɛxt] ) is the fourth-largest city of the Netherlands, as well as the capital and the most populous city of the province of Utrecht. The municipality of Utrecht is located in the eastern part of the Randstad conurbation, and had a population of 361,924 as of 1 December 2021.
Utrecht's ancient city centre features many buildings and structures, several dating as far back as the High Middle Ages.

== Etymology ==
The name Utrecht is derived from the Latin Ultraiectum, meaning "beyond the ford", referring to a crossing of the Rhine.

== History ==

=== Prehistory and Roman times ===
Although there is some evidence of earlier inhabitation in the region, the city of Utrecht dates back to the Roman fort of Traiectum, built around 50 AD.
The fort was part of the northern border of the Roman Empire, the Limes Germanicus.

=== Middle Ages ===
In 1122, Utrecht was granted city rights by Emperor Henry V.
The Dom Tower, completed in 1382, is the tallest church tower in the country.
A tornado in 1674 destroyed the nave of the cathedral, which was never rebuilt.

=== Population ===
In 2020 the city had 357,179 inhabitants, with a population density of 3,600 per km2.

== Demographics ==
About 54% of the population is of Dutch descent.

== Culture ==
Every summer the city hosts the Festival Oude Muziek, one of the most renowned festivals of early music in the world.

== Twin towns ==
Utrecht is twinned with Brno and León.
